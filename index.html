Collecting and Using Your Personal Data

Types of Data Collected

Personal Data

While using Our Service, We may ask You to provide Us with certain personally identifiable information that can be used to contact or identify You. Personally identifiable information may include, but is not limited to:

  • First name and last name (optional, stored locally if you choose to set a profile name)
  • Email address (only if you voluntarily sign in with Google for backup/restore)
  • Usage Data

Important: Nidhi does not collect your financial transaction data (amounts, categories, accounts). All financial records remain in the local Room database on your device. We have no servers that store your income or expense information.

Google Sign-In (Optional)

If you choose to enable backup and restore via Google Drive, Nidhi uses Google Sign-In. When you sign in, we receive your name, email address, and profile picture URL. Your transaction data is never stored on our servers — it remains on your device or inside your private Google Drive (if you initiate backup). You may sign out at any time, and all backup data stays under your Google account control.

Usage Data

Usage Data is collected automatically when using the Service. This may include information such as Your Device's Internet Protocol address (e.g. IP address), device type, operating system version, unique device identifiers, diagnostic data, and interaction with the app (e.g., feature usage). We use this data only to improve stability and performance; we do not sell or share it with advertisers.

Screenshot Import (Premium Feature – On‑Device OCR)

When You use the screenshot import feature, images are processed locally using on-device Optical Character Recognition (OCR). No image or extracted text is ever uploaded to any external server. The extracted data (amount, date, description) is only used to pre‑fill the transaction form for your convenience.

QR Code Data Transfer (Device‑to‑Device)

Nidhi allows you to transfer transaction data between devices using QR codes. This transfer occurs directly between devices — no intermediate servers or cloud storage are involved. The receiving device reconstructs the data locally.
